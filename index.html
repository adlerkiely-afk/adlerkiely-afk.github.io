2027 IREC payload: pitot deployment mechanism

One unit of the team's CubeSat-format weather station payload. A single servo drives a symmetrical linkage that pushes the pitot probes out of the payload during descent.

Mechanism trade studyAssembly modeled in OnshapeBeing builtLaunches at IREC, summer 2027
  • The hard part: getting symmetric deployment from one servo, inside a CubeSat-format frame.
  • The change of plan: the probes were first meant to measure wind speed. We determined that reading could not be accurate, so the design was re-approached to measure descent speed and give the weather station more data against altitude.
  • Trade study: rack-and-pinion against a slider-crank, with stroke and torque sizing.

Independent project · 2026 – present

SpeedUAS: high-speed quadcopter

A 5-inch, 6S quadcopter with a streamlined body-of-revolution fuselage. The goal of this first iteration is to validate the airframe and set a baseline for a second one.

Airframe modeled in SolidWorksFit-check printsCFD meshing, not yet solvedNot assembled or flown
  • Fuselage: Myring body of revolution, 62 mm diameter, with the battery forward and the flight electronics aft so the frontal area stays small.
  • Arms: four integral NACA 0024 arms, 38 mm chord, with a carbon-fibre spar and internal wire channels. A thinner NACA 0018 was considered and rejected: slightly less drag, but far less bending stiffness and no room for the spar and wiring.
  • Estimate so far: a thrust-equals-drag model predicts about 240 km/h against a 160–175 km/h target. It uses datasheet thrust and assumed drag coefficients, so it is an estimate, not a result. In that model, top speed depends on pitch speed far more than on drag.

What the first prints taught me

The battery didn't fit

I modeled the pack from its datasheet size with about 1 mm of clearance. The real pack measured 34.9 × 38.7 × 80.7 mm. The fuselage diameter went up and the cage was resized to the measured part.

I misread a datasheet

I drew the motor bolt pattern as a 16 mm square. The "16" was the bolt circle. Measuring the motor fixed the pattern and let the nacelles shrink to the motor.

A bridge that printed badly

The motor plate was a 29 mm unsupported bridge. It is now a funnel-shaped cavity with screw pockets, and every part prints without supports.

Kent State Rocket Team · 2026 – present

2027 IREC payload: pitot deployment mechanism

One unit of the team's CubeSat-format weather station payload. A single servo drives a symmetrical linkage that pushes the pitot probes out of the payload during descent.

  • Mechanism trade study
  • Modeled in Onshape
  • Being built
  • Launches at IREC, summer 2027
The hard part
Getting symmetric deployment from one servo, inside a CubeSat-format frame.
Change of plan
The probes were first meant to measure wind speed. We determined that reading could not be accurate, so the design was re-approached to measure descent speed and give the weather station more data against altitude.
Trade study
Rack-and-pinion against a slider-crank, with stroke and torque sizing.
My role
Payload Subsection Lead, directing a 6-member subsection through design, fabrication and integration.
Status
Not flown yet. No flight data.
02
Independent project · 2026 – present

SpeedUAS: high-speed quadcopter

A 5-inch, 6S quadcopter with a streamlined body-of-revolution fuselage. The goal of this first iteration is to validate the airframe and set a baseline for a second one.

  • Modeled in SolidWorks
  • Fit-check prints
  • CFD meshing, not yet solved
  • Not assembled or flown
Fuselage
Myring body of revolution, 62 mm diameter, with the battery forward and the flight electronics aft so the frontal area stays small.
Arms
Four integral NACA 0024 arms, 38 mm chord, with a carbon-fibre spar and internal wire channels. A thinner NACA 0018 was considered and rejected: slightly less drag, but far less bending stiffness and no room for the spar and wiring.
Estimate so far
A thrust-equals-drag model predicts about 240 km/h against a 160–175 km/h target. It uses datasheet thrust and assumed drag coefficients, so it is an estimate, not a result.
Tools
SolidWorks · Ansys (SpaceClaim, Fluent Meshing) · Bambu Studio · calipers
Status
In development. Nothing here has been flown or measured in the air. Repository

What the first prints taught me

SpeedUAS
i.

The battery didn't fit

I modeled the pack from its datasheet size with about 1 mm of clearance. The real pack measured 34.9 × 38.7 × 80.7 mm. The fuselage diameter went up and the cage was resized to the measured part.

ii.

I misread a datasheet

I drew the motor bolt pattern as a 16 mm square. The "16" was the bolt circle. Measuring the motor fixed the pattern and let the nacelles shrink to the motor.

iii.

A bridge that printed badly

The motor plate was a 29 mm unsupported bridge. It is now a funnel-shaped cavity with screw pockets, and every part prints without supports.

Next on SpeedUAS

In order
1

Fit print of the current center tube

A pass/fail check of the bore, the stack plate, the insert holes and the tail fit.

2

Weigh every part

Measured masses in place of datasheet values, for the first real all-up mass and thrust-to-weight figure.

3

Solve the CFD

Finish the volume mesh, solve the bare airframe, and replace the estimated drag area with a computed one. Then run the thrust stand, which is designed and printed but not yet run.

Kent State Rocket Team · 2027 IREC payload

Four probes out of one payload, with one servo.

This is one unit of the team's 4U CubeSat-format weather station payload. During descent, a single servo drives a symmetrical linkage that pushes the pitot probes out of the payload.

The probes were first meant to measure wind speed. We decided that reading couldn't be accurate, so we re-approached the design to measure descent speed, which gives the weather station more data against altitude.

Independent project · April 2026 – present

A quadcopter shaped like a rocket.

A 5-inch, 6S quadcopter with a Myring body-of-revolution fuselage, 62 mm in diameter. The battery sits forward and the flight electronics aft, which keeps the frontal area small. This first iteration is meant to validate the airframe and set a baseline for a second one.

The four integral NACA 0024 arms (38 mm chord) carry a carbon-fibre spar and internal wire channels. I considered a thinner NACA 0018 and rejected it: slightly less drag, but far less bending stiffness and no room for the spar and wiring.

A thrust-equals-drag model predicts about 240 km/h against a 160–175 km/h target. It uses datasheet thrust and assumed drag coefficients, so it's an estimate, not a result.

Lesson i
The battery didn't fit

I modeled the pack from its datasheet with about 1 mm of clearance. The real pack measured 34.9 × 38.7 × 80.7 mm, so the fuselage grew and the cage was resized to the measured part.

Lesson ii
I misread a datasheet

I drew the motor bolt pattern as a 16 mm square, but the "16" was the bolt circle. Measuring the motor fixed it and let the nacelles shrink to the motor.

Lesson iii
A bridge that printed badly

A 29 mm unsupported bridge on the motor plate became a funnel-shaped cavity with screw pockets. Every part now prints without supports.

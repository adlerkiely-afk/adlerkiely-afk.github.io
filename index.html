Pitot deployment mechanism

Payload Subsection Lead · 6-member subsection · Aug 2026 to present · In design

Payload form factor
4UCubeSat

One servo turns a crank that drives a carriage, pushing straight pitot probes out of the bottom of a CubeSat-format payload.

The team's payload for the 2027 rocket is a weather station in a 4U CubeSat format. My part is the unit that deploys the pitot probes. During descent, one servo turns a crank that drives a carriage down two guide rods and pushes straight probes out through the bottom of the payload.

The probes were first meant to measure wind speed from the sides. We decided that reading couldn't be accurate, so the probes now measure descent speed, which gives the weather station more data against altitude.

My first mechanism pushed four probes out of the side walls, and each tube had to be bent to face down. The current revision points straight probes down, so there is no bend. Its CAD has three probes and the count is still open. I printed both designs in PLA+ and ran them on the servo. The servo mount isn't designed yet, and the revision hasn't been through team review. The project page shows both designs.

I'm using FEA to choose a material for the linkage. I'm checking it against hand calculations of the servo stall loads, starting with the link. No material is chosen yet.

SpeedUAS quadcopter

A 5-inch, 6S quadcopter with a streamlined body-of-revolution fuselage and airfoil arms.

SpeedUAS is a 5-inch, 6S quadcopter with a Myring body-of-revolution fuselage, 62 mm in diameter. The battery sits forward and the flight electronics aft, which keeps the frontal area small. This is the first iteration. I'm using it to validate the airframe and to set a baseline for a second one.

The four arms are NACA 0024 sections with a 38 mm chord, integral with the airframe. Each carries a carbon-fibre spar and internal wire channels. I considered a thinner NACA 0018 and rejected it. It had slightly less drag, but far less bending stiffness and no room for the spar and wiring.

A thrust-equals-drag model predicts about 240 km/h against a target of 160 to 175 km/h. The model uses datasheet thrust and assumed drag coefficients, so the 240 is an estimate and I have no test data behind it.

  • Battery bay

    The battery didn't fit

    I modeled the pack from its datasheet with about 1 mm of clearance. The real pack measured 34.9 × 38.7 × 80.7 mm, so the fuselage grew and I resized the cage to the measured part.

  • Motor mount

    I misread a datasheet

    I drew the motor bolt pattern as a 16 mm square, but the "16" was the bolt circle. Measuring the motor fixed it and let the nacelles shrink to the motor.

  • Motor plate

    A bridge that printed badly

    The motor plate had a 29 mm unsupported bridge. I replaced it with a funnel-shaped cavity with screw pockets. Every part now prints without supports.
